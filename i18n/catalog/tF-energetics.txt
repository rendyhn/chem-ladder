<h3>Bond enthalpies</h3>
<p>Breaking a bond takes energy (endothermic); making a bond gives it out (exothermic). The <b>bond enthalpy</b> is the energy needed to break 1 mol of a bond in the gas state, averaged over many compounds.</p>

<p>Many reactions are <b>reversible</b> ($\rightleftharpoons$): the products can react to give back the reactants. In a closed system the forward reaction slows down as reactants are used up, the backward reaction speeds up as products build up, until both rates are equal. This is <b>dynamic equilibrium</b>: both reactions keep going, but the concentrations no longer change.</p>

<p>For $a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \rightleftharpoons c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}$ the <b>equilibrium constant</b> is $$K_c = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c[\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a[\mathrm{B}]^b}$$ using equilibrium concentrations (pure solids and liquids are left out). For gases, $K_p$ uses partial pressures, and $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$. $K \gg 1$: mostly products; $K \ll 1$: mostly reactants. $K$ changes only with temperature.</p>
